# Agent37 vs Daytona: results (run Nov 3, 2026, 12:16–1:05 PM PT)

All numbers regenerate from committed data: `python3 analysis/analyze.py` → `out/tables.md`; `analysis/spend.py`, `analysis/billing.py`.
384 grid sandboxes (48 per cell, 8 cells), 320 burst creates plus a 55-create over-limit probe, 6 sleep/wake instances, plus one 24 h soak instance per provider. No LLM calls.

## Headlines
- **Price:** Agent37 default is $0.0070/h (2/4/8) and $0.0133/h (4/8/10) against Daytona's $0.166 and $0.332, about 24–25x less. Agent37 Performance (dedicated cores) is still about 6.6x cheaper than Daytona.
- **This workload:** the matched default cells (96 sandboxes each) cost $0.051 on Agent37 (whole minutes) against $1.54 on Daytona (per second), about 30x. Part of that gap is Daytona's slower full clones in this run. All 6 Agent37 cells (288 sandboxes) cost $0.42.
- **Startup:** Daytona create returns in about 0.22–0.26 s, Agent37 in about 1.2–1.3 s for every type. Time to first command is similar (0.28–0.46 s); the Agent37 VM is slowest.
- **CPU:** Agent37 default shares and overcommits cores. Under 180 s of full load its slowest 5 s window was about 0.63–0.68 of its fastest, and the median 2 vCPU rate was about 15% below Daytona's. Agent37 Performance (0.89–0.93) and Daytona (0.93) hold steady; the Agent37 VM is in between.
- **Memory:** every type except Agent37 default stops the process at or just below the nominal RAM (exit 137) and the sandbox survives. Agent37 default lets a process go past nominal (4.4 GiB on 4, 8.9 GiB on 8) and then, in 4 of 5 Medium runs and 1 of 5 Large, the whole sandbox restarts: `/tmp` is wiped and the API answers "instance is not running" for a while.
- **Disk:** the home volume fills to exactly the requested size on default and Performance. Agent37 adds a separate 10 GB system layer, so a default sandbox can write up to 10.7 GB outside home. Agent37 VMs got about 10.4 GB for both shapes; the requested 8 GB was not applied.
- **Bursts:** 0 errors in 320 creates on either provider at 10, 25 and 45 at once. Agent37's median create time rose to about 1.8–2.0 s at 45, with p95 4–5 s; Daytona stayed at about 0.4 s, with p95 about 2 s. At 55 against Agent37's 50-instance limit, 3 got `409 instance_limit_reached`, but 52 were created, so the limit check overshoots under concurrency.
- **Billing:** Agent37 billed $0.4835 for 325 grid and pilot instances, against $0.4849 modeled with whole minutes and $0.4174 modeled by the second. It rounds each sandbox up to whole minutes, which matches its docs. The workspace's total compute was $0.510.
- **Sleep/wake:** Agent37 wakes to the first command in a median 0.56 s, Daytona stop/start in 1.38 s. Files survived 15/15 on both. A background process started over exec survived 0/12 on both, even though Agent37 documents that in-memory state "usually survives".
- **24 h soak:** one Medium instance per provider, put to sleep (Agent37) or stopped (Daytona) and woken with one command every 30 min, Nov 3 12:16 PM to Nov 4 12:49 PM PT. Our harness box was paused overnight, so wakes were skipped for 11.6–12.5 h and then 3.5–4.5 h; the instances stayed parked through those gaps. The marker file was there on every successful wake (Agent37 16/16, Daytona 11/11). Agent37 woke in a median 0.79 s (0.59–1.09 s), with 1 exec timeout and 2 sleep-call timeouts. Daytona woke in a median 1.47 s (1.18–1.60 s), but 5 of 16 wake attempts failed (3 `start` calls past 900 s, 2 exec read timeouts), and 2 stops were refused while it was stuck. The soak checked files only; background processes did not survive sleep in the short sleep/wake test (0/12 on both). Modeled 24.5 h parked cost at published disk-only rates is about $0.024 on Agent37 and $0.008 on Daytona, against $0.17 and $4.07 always-on. Both instances were deleted by the harness at the end.
- **Clones (caveat):** full clones ran 40–50 at a time against GitHub from each provider's shared outbound addresses. Agent37 default and Performance finished full clones in a median 90–175 s; Daytona and Agent37 VM took 510–680 s. Shallow clones were the same everywhere (23–35 s). The full-clone gap likely reflects how GitHub treats each provider's outbound addresses at that concurrency, not raw sandbox speed; earlier one-at-a-time Daytona clones took 116–184 s.

## Failures kept
- 61 attempts lost to our harness: a synchronous exec that stays silent for about 270 s loses its HTTP connection on both providers, and the Daytona SDK then re-sends the command. They are in `results/runs_full_harness_failed/` and were re-run with probes detached and polled.
- Agent37 VM Medium: one sandbox's first command hung for 870 s right after create, which is a provider-side failure.
- Full clones over 25 min: 1 Agent37 VM Large, 1 Agent37 VM Medium, 1 Daytona Large.
